# Ties 0.2.0 — your Mac already knows who they are

0.1 researched the people in your address book from what the open web says. 0.2 adds what your own
Mac already knows about them — how you and the people in your chats address them, what they wrote
under their own emails, the name they gave themselves on WhatsApp, the links they sent you — and
uses it to search better, to pick the right person, and to tell you when you last talked.

Everything below is in this build. What moved between the roadmap post and the release is in *What
changed from the roadmap*.

## Sources: chats and mail become evidence

A new **Sources** step in the wizard, mirrored in Settings › Sources. Four rows, four icons, four
toggles: Contacts, Messages, WhatsApp, Mail. Contacts needs nothing extra; the other three need
Full Disk Access once.

- **Known as.** The nickname you saved, the display name they set on WhatsApp, the name their
  colleagues use for them in your group chats — and the honorific in front of it. "Dr.", "Eng.",
  "Prof.", "دكتور", "مهندس" are read in both languages, diacritics and the definite article and
  all.
- **Signatures.** Title, company, phone, website, LinkedIn, parsed out of the block under their own
  mail, in three layouts, with quoted replies and newsletters rejected.
- **Links they shared.** A LinkedIn, GitHub or X URL they sent you themselves, canonicalised.
- **When you last talked, and how often.** Across Messages, WhatsApp and Mail.

Reading is read-only and off the live file: the store is copied to a temporary folder, opened
read-only, capped at the last 500 messages a person and the 50 most recent mails an address, and
the copy is deleted when the pass ends. Nothing is written back to any source.

## Verification that finally uses what only you have

Every signal feeds the candidate scorer. A profile at a URL the person shared or signed with is
accepted on the spot when something on it corroborates them — the page names them, or the URL
itself spells a name they go by; a link they merely forwarded lands on the review screen instead of
being taken as proof. A headline matching a signature title climbs. A namesake whose profession
contradicts their honorific or their signature sinks. The city from a postal address or a signature
finally makes 0.1's location weight live, and contacts with nothing but a name and a phone number
are now searched with their real title and company as seeds.

## Several times faster

- **A search pool.** Two hidden web views by default, up to four in Settings › Research, each
  pacing itself with its own jitter. Scanner concurrency follows the pool size.
- **Engine failover.** When one engine starts asking a view to prove it is not a robot, that view
  moves to another engine for ten minutes instead of backing off.
- **Skip the obvious.** When a shared link already settles the identity, Ties fetches that page and
  skips the search.
- **A hard budget per person in Quick mode** — 25 seconds, whatever finished is scored.

## AI where an assistant would sit

Never in the way, always marked with a small sparkle, always your choice of provider, on-device
first.

- **Judge.** For a person with more than one plausible match, the provider reads the evidence and
  says which one, in a line under 90 characters. You still click; nothing is auto-accepted.
- **Smart lists.** Your network grouped in the sidebar — Doctors, Founders, Engineers in Riyadh —
  at most eight, rebuilt on demand.
- **Ask, better.** "Help with taxes" becomes accountant, CPA, tax advisor, bookkeeper, shown as
  chips you can remove; a removed chip is a term the search never sees.
- **Draft.** Say what you need; Ties writes a short message and opens Messages, WhatsApp or Mail
  with it prefilled. Nothing is sent by Ties.
- **Reconnect.** People whose profile answers what you are looking for and who you have not talked
  to in three months, strongest tie first, one click from Draft.
- **Fact check.** Every extracted fact is re-checked against the pages it cites; the ones those
  pages do not support get a dotted underline, not your trust.

## What changed from the roadmap

Three things are not quite what the roadmap post described, and it is worth saying so plainly.

**Only two engines work.** The roadmap said the pool rotates to Bing or Brave. It rotates to
Yahoo. A spike ran each candidate engine against a real query before the failover was wired:
DuckDuckGo returned ten usable results, Yahoo seven; Bing returned ten rows of results unrelated to
the query, every link behind a `bing.com/ck/a` redirector, and an outright bot wall once the market
was pinned; Brave served a bot-verification interstitial with no results markup at all; Mojeek
answered `403 — your network appears to be sending automated queries`. Bing, Brave and Mojeek ship
in the build **disabled**, with their URL templates, selectors and link decoders kept, so
re-enabling any of them is a one-word change once they answer again. Yahoo was verified on one good
load, from one network, on one day; if it stops answering, DuckDuckGo carries the work and the
`scripts/spike-engines.swift` script re-checks it in one command.

**The judge sends less than "the evidence".** It receives the candidates' public page snippets plus
exactly four kinds of local signal — aliases, titles, companies and honorifics — and only when the
provider is on-device or you have turned the privacy switch on. A code review during the build
found it was also carrying the links a person shared and their city; both were cut, in the prompt
builder itself, so no future caller can put them back by accident.

**Draft learns how you text, but only where it is safe to.** Ties reads your own last twenty
messages to that person, from your 1:1 chats, and uses them to match your register. That sample goes
to an on-device model freely; it reaches a cloud provider only if you turned on "Let cloud AI see
local signals", and otherwise the message comes back in a neutral register. Either way the draft
opens in Messages, WhatsApp or Mail for you to edit, and Ties never sends it.

Everything else in the roadmap post is in the build.

## Full Disk Access

Messages, WhatsApp and Mail live in places macOS protects. On the Sources step, or in Settings ›
Sources, click **Open Privacy Settings**, switch **Ties** on under Privacy & Security › Full Disk
Access, and come back — macOS may ask to quit and reopen the app. Contacts needs nothing beyond the
permission 0.1 already asked for. You can revoke Full Disk Access at any time; Ties keeps working
with what the web says, and the rows go back to showing a lock.

## Install

Download `Ties.dmg` from the release, open it, drag **Ties** to **Applications**. The build is
unsigned, so the first launch needs **right-click → Open** and then *Open* in the dialog (or
`xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Ties.app` once). Requires macOS 15 or later; Apple
Intelligence as a provider needs macOS 26 on a supported Mac. An existing 0.1 database is migrated
in place; nothing is re-researched, and Settings › Sources › **Collect again** is what reads your
chats and mail into it. If you use the Codex or Gemini CLI as your provider, update it
first: Ties now asks every provider for a schema-constrained answer and passes flags that older CLI
builds do not understand.

## Privacy

What Ties learns from your chats and mail stays on this Mac, with two deliberate exceptions, both of
which are the feature working. Searching: a name, an employer, a job title or another name someone
goes by can appear in a web search, the same way a contact's name and company already did in 0.1 —
that is how a person is found at all. The AI: with "Let cloud AI see local signals" switched off, a
cloud provider is sent only what your address book already held; switch it on and it also sees
aliases, titles, companies and honorifics, plus — only when you ask for a draft — your own last
twenty messages to that person. It is never sent a message anyone sent you, a subject line, or your
address book. Ties opens your Messages, WhatsApp and Mail stores read-only, from a temporary
copy, and never modifies them. Full Disk Access can be revoked at any time; Ties keeps working with
what the web says. On-device Apple Intelligence sees everything and sends nothing. Everything still
lives in one SQLite file you can delete, and Ties still never logs in anywhere and never writes to
Apple Contacts.
